Start with these three cards

Answer from memory before revealing the example. Say what the method measures and what the result does not establish.

Methods / 1 min

How does Sanger sequencing work?

A primer defines where the read starts. An incorporated fluorescently labelled dideoxynucleotide (ddNTP) lacks a 3′-OH, so that strand cannot extend. Separating the fragments by length and reading their terminal labels gives the sequence of the newly synthesised strand. NHGRI: DNA sequencing

Model answer and follow-up
Sanger sequencing uses chain-terminating nucleotides. When a labelled ddNTP is incorporated, the strand stops growing because it has no 3′ hydroxyl group. The instrument separates the fragments by length and reads the label on each fragment to infer the sequence.

Follow-up: Do two overlapping peaks prove a mutation? No. Check read quality and consider mixed templates; the trace alone may not establish the cause.

Cloning / 1 min

How can a DNA fragment become part of a plasmid?

Compare the principle and the reason for choosing a method. Restriction–ligation relies on compatible ends; Gibson assembly uses overlaps; Golden Gate uses type IIS enzymes and defined overhangs. Which approach suits the question depends in part on the number of fragments and the intended junctions. Use a fictional reporter example, not a protocol. Addgene: cloning methods

Model answer and follow-up
I would distinguish restriction–ligation, overlap-based assembly such as Gibson, and type IIS assembly such as Golden Gate. My choice would depend on the number of fragments and what the junctions need to look like. Detecting an insert isn't the same as confirming its orientation, sequence or function.

Follow-up: Is detecting the DNA insert evidence of expression? The DNA identifies part of the construct. Expression requires an appropriate RNA or protein readout; a functional effect on its own does not identify which construct caused it.

Motivation / 45 s

Why EMBL? Give a specific scientific reason

Explain what you would do with access to specialists, colleagues from different disciplines and the chance to plan analysis before collecting data. You can mention the Thesis Advisory Committee without assuming that a particular group has already assigned you a bioinformatics mentor. EMBL: programme and training · EMBL: Core Facilities

Model answer
I’m drawn to EMBL because I want to work alongside scientists who approach the same question from different angles. I’d like to bring my experimental background and learn to design the analysis alongside the experiments. To me, state-of-the-art research means being able to get expert input early, choose the right method and then take responsibility for what the data can actually tell us.

Methods: panel follow-ups

These cards are always available. Explain the principle with a neutral example. When discussing your own work, say only what you did and observed.

T01 / interpretation

Sanger: a clean trace, but an unexpected base

A clear peak supports a base call. Comparing the read with the expected sequence reveals a discrepancy; the read alone does not establish its cause or verify the rest of the construct. Addgene: sequence verification

Follow-up and model answer

The trace is clean, but one high-quality base differs from the expected sequence. What can you conclude?

The read supports a difference at that position, but it doesn’t tell me why it’s there. A clean trace is evidence of read quality, not a guarantee that the sequence matches the design or that the rest of the construct is correct.
T02 / mechanism

Gibson assembly: what do the three enzymes do?

An exonuclease exposes single-stranded regions that can pair with complementary overlaps. Polymerase fills gaps, and ligase seals nicks in the backbone. Golden Gate uses compatible overhangs produced by a type IIS enzyme that cuts outside its recognition sequence. Gibson et al. · Engler et al.

Model answer and comparison
Gibson uses overlaps to bring the right fragments together. An exonuclease exposes complementary ends, a polymerase fills gaps, and a ligase seals the remaining nicks.

What determines the order of the fragments in Gibson assembly, and how is that different in Golden Gate?

P03 / evidence and limitation

How would you verify a clone?

Junction PCR: an expected-size product supports the junction if the assay is specific and the controls are interpretable. The band does not identify its sequence or verify regions elsewhere. A sequence read checks the region it covers; expression and function are separate claims. Addgene: verification methods

Model answer and a worked follow-up
A junction PCR can support the presence of the expected junction. It doesn’t establish the exact sequence, so I’d distinguish the PCR result from sequence verification and both from evidence of function.

In an example from your research, what did each readout support? Would an expected-size band and an unexpected base in the same region contradict each other?

Read more in the English handbook: Sanger traces, Gibson and Golden Gate or clone verification.

Three extra cards: interpreting evidence

Available now, alongside the timed cards. Practise each answer aloud before opening it. Read the chapter and worked examples.

E01 / joint measurements

Sites A and B are each occupied on half of the DNA molecules. Must they be occupied together?

Model answer and follow-up
No. They might be on the same half or on entirely different halves. I would need an observation that preserves which sites are occupied on each molecule.

Follow-up: Does co-occupancy prove contact between the proteins? No. Two proteins can occupy the same molecule without directly interacting.

E02 / confounding

Could a classifier distinguish treatments because it learnt the imaging day?

Model answer and follow-up
Yes. If all controls were acquired on one day and treatments on another, treatment and day cannot be separated in those data. A random split of cells from the same wells would not solve that.

Follow-up: What would make validation stronger? Include both conditions across independent batches and hold out whole groups that match the intended use.

E03 / pools and rates

Does an unchanged metabolite concentration mean unchanged metabolic flux?

Model answer and follow-up
No. At fixed volume, production and consumption may both rise while remaining balanced. I have measured how much is present, not the individual rates.

Follow-up: Does higher final density under stress prove protection? No. Start with unstressed baselines and define what outcome and scale you are comparing.

25.11 · 08:00

Sanger: a single sequence or a mixture?

Why does a clean trace for one read not verify the whole molecule? If two peaks overlap at one position, check the local quality and consider whether more than one template contributed.

A clean trace supports the sequence of the region I actually read. It doesn’t prove that regions outside the read are correct.

Follow-up: What evidence would distinguish a real mixed template from a low-quality trace?

25.11 · 18:15

Insert, orientation, sequence and function

These are four different claims. Name a measurement that addresses each one. A functional readout does not automatically verify insert orientation or the expected DNA sequence.

I would use screening to identify candidates and sequence evidence to verify what is actually present. A functional readout answers a separate question.
26.11 · 08:00

qPCR: what does an earlier Cq tell you?

Cq is estimated from the amplification curve, often from a fluorescence threshold crossing. An earlier Cq can indicate more starting target if the assay and conditions are comparable. Cq alone does not give a copy count.

I would first check whether the assays and samples are comparable before turning a Cq difference into a quantitative conclusion.

Follow-up: What would a signal in a no-template control make you question?

26.11 · 18:15

Location, abundance and sequence are different claims

FISH can locate a signal, qPCR can estimate the amount of a chosen target, and sequencing reveals the bases in the region covered. Choose a readout that addresses your claim. Detecting a signal is not proof of a stable genomic change.

These readouts answer different questions: where the target is, how much we detect and what sequence is present.
27.11 · 08:00

ELISA: concentration in the medium is not the production rate

Extracellular concentration can change because of production, release, loss or the number of contributing cells. Choose one figure and name the measurement, time point and comparator.

The assay measures how much cytokine was detectable in the medium. On its own it doesn’t tell us how much the cells produced.
27.11 · 18:15

Annexin V and PI: what do these markers show?

Annexin V reports accessible phosphatidylserine; propidium iodide signal is associated with loss of membrane integrity. The quadrant alone does not establish a cell-death mechanism. Koopman et al.: Annexin V

I would describe marker-positive populations before assigning a cell-death mechanism.
28.11 · 08:00

Two significance tests, one missing comparison

If A differs significantly from control but B does not, you still need a direct A–B comparison to assess whether they differ. Explain what the contrast and its uncertainty would tell you. Gelman and Stern: interpreting significance

A significant result in one comparison and a non-significant result in another don’t establish a difference between the two effects.
28.11 · 18:15

Western blot: a band is not a mechanism

Explain why a reference is used for normalisation and what the signal tells you relative to it. A change in detectable protein abundance does not by itself establish activity or identify the cause.

The blot supports a change in detectable abundance under those conditions. It is not a direct measurement of activity.
29.11 · 08:00

Turn a broad question into a testable comparison

Name the conditions, comparison and measured outcome. If both groups move in the same direction, ask whether the difference between them has changed. Report the estimate and its uncertainty.

I would ask whether the between-group difference changed, rather than treating any change in both groups as evidence for the hypothesis.
29.11 · 18:15

Bioinformatics: explain a decision, not a tool list

Describe the data, one check you ran, what you found and what decision followed. Then name one part of an analysis you would like to lead. Experience and plans for training are separate.

I can describe one analysis I’ve actually done, then explain which part of a larger analysis I’d like to own during the PhD.

Describe a problem and your contribution

Rather than reciting a protocol, describe a control or observation that changed your interpretation. If the question is unresolved, say what the evidence supports and what remains open.

My first interpretation was … The control made me reconsider because … What I can say now is …

How would you close the EMBL interview?

Ask something the programme page cannot answer. For example, ask how students take responsibility for their analyses and who gives them feedback. Do not assume a project or collaboration has already been agreed.

How do students who combine experiments and computation get feedback on their analysis early in a project?
