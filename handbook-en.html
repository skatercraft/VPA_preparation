Chapter 01

Sanger sequencing and trace interpretation

The question this method answers: What is the sequence of the dominant template represented in this read, and how confidently can I call the bases in the region I care about?

A primer provides a starting point for DNA polymerase. Ordinary deoxynucleotides leave a 3′ hydroxyl group that supports continued extension. A dideoxynucleotide (ddNTP) lacks that group, so no further nucleotide can be attached to that strand. Different copies stop at different positions. The fluorescent tag identifies the terminating base; the tag is not what stops the chain. The result is a set of fragments sharing one starting point but ending at successive positions.

From fluorescence to a sequence

Capillary electrophoresis separates the products by length. As fragments reach the detector, fluorescence is recorded in channels associated with the four bases. The base caller infers the sequence from the order and quality of the peaks. The called sequence describes the newly synthesised strand in the 5′→3′ direction. A read from the opposite orientation must be compared in the equivalent orientation, typically by taking its reverse complement. The short segment shown here is fictional and not a reference for dye colours or peak heights.

The chromatogram is evidence, not just a decorative version of the base calls. A clean but unexpected peak at a well-resolved position supports a discrepancy with the expected sequence. It does not identify the cause of the discrepancy. A single ambiguous position can reflect noise or multiple templates; a long stretch of overlapping, shifted peaks can be consistent with a mixture of templates differing by an insertion or deletion. A clean trace does not exclude a sufficiently rare variant, verify a second junction outside the read, or establish the sequence of every molecule in the tube.

Worked case. A construct gives an expected-size band but a clear base substitution in the read. These observations are compatible. The band reports approximate size; replacing one base with another need not change that size. Ask whether the position is well resolved, whether the comparison uses the right sequence and orientation, and whether independent evidence supports a discrepancy. Do not call the clone entirely correct or infer why the difference arose.

Panel question and short answer

Question: How does Sanger sequencing work, and what can you infer from a clean trace?

A ddNTP stops extension because it lacks a 3′ hydroxyl. The instrument separates fragments by length and reads the dye on each terminal base. A clean trace supports the dominant sequence in the high-quality region. I would still compare it with the expected sequence.

Read further: Sanger et al.: chain termination · Prober et al.: fluorescent terminators · Yale sequencing core: interpreting difficult traces.

Chapter 02

Gibson assembly and other assembly logic

The question: What makes the intended pieces meet in the intended order, and what remains to be verified afterwards?

In Gibson assembly, overlaps encode which fragments are neighbours. A 5′ exonuclease exposes complementary single-stranded regions. Matching regions can anneal. DNA polymerase fills gaps where bases are missing, and DNA ligase seals remaining nicks in the backbone. A gap is a stretch of missing nucleotides; a nick is a break between adjacent nucleotides in one strand. In an interview, explaining that distinction shows more understanding than listing the three enzymes alone.

Compare the source of specificity

  • Restriction and ligation: compatible DNA ends created by cleavage permit joining. Two different, non-interchangeable ends can constrain orientation; identical compatible ends generally do not enforce it on their own.
  • Gibson: overlapping complementary regions guide joining. The junction need not contain a particular restriction recognition site. A junction described as scarless does not have to acquire extra bases merely because the fragments were joined. It is not a certificate of sequence accuracy.
  • Golden Gate: a type IIS enzyme recognises one sequence and cuts outside it. The resulting compatible overhangs can specify the order and orientation of parts. The recognition sequence and the joining interface are distinct ideas.

A useful comparison starts with the design question, not a claim that one method is universally best. Is the main constraint the sequence of the junction, reuse of a standard set of pieces, or working with several parts? If an assembly is intended to create a reporting construct, its sequence and its readout are still separate claims. An observed output does not automatically tell you that all joints match the intended map.

Panel question and short answer

Question: What is the difference between Gibson and Golden Gate?

Gibson uses sequence overlaps. An exonuclease exposes them, polymerase fills gaps, and ligase seals nicks. In Golden Gate, type IIS enzymes cut outside their recognition sites and the overhangs define which pieces join.

Read further: Gibson et al.: overlapping DNA fragments · Engler et al.: type IIS assembly · Addgene: comparison of cloning techniques.

Chapter 03

PCR, gels and clone verification

The difference between a signal and an identification

A conventional PCR readout depends on whether DNA compatible with the assay is amplified. The position of a band on a gel gives an approximate size relative to a marker. It is not a base-by-base identification. A product of a plausible size can be non-specific; an absent band can mean either absence of the target or failure to detect it. A junction PCR is informative about the particular boundary it interrogates, to the extent that the assay distinguishes it from other possibilities. Detecting one boundary does not establish the rest of the construct.

An evidence map, not a ladder

Start with the claim and select a measurement that actually bears on it. A screen can narrow the set of candidates. Sequence alignment can assess the covered region and report discrepancies or gaps. Evidence for expression may come from an appropriate RNA or protein readout. A functional assay addresses a biological outcome, but a positive outcome alone does not identify which molecular change caused it. These are complementary measurements, not four levels where the last automatically proves the first three.

Worked case. A hypothetical gel shows the expected-size product for one junction and no product for a second. If the second assay also fails on the known positive control, that negative sample result is not informative. If the positive control performs as expected, the sample remains a candidate with incomplete evidence, not a verified full-length construct. A clean sequence read covering the first junction only still leaves the second unresolved.

Panel question and short answer

Question: Does a correctly sized band confirm the clone?

It supports a candidate with the expected product size, provided the assay and its controls are interpretable. It does not establish the exact sequence, structure elsewhere, or function.

Read further: Addgene: what colony PCR can screen for · Addgene: sequence interpretation.

Chapter 04

qPCR, RT-qPCR and normalisation

What does Cq mean?

In qPCR, an instrument follows fluorescence associated with amplification. The quantification cycle (Cq) is a value estimated on the cycle scale from that curve. In a threshold-based analysis, it is the cycle at which the curve crosses the threshold; not every instrument or algorithm uses that definition. Under comparable analysis and measurement conditions, an earlier Cq for the same assay is consistent with more initial target. Efficiency and inhibition can also shift curves. Endpoint yield and starting quantity are not interchangeable, and comparing uncalibrated Cq values across unrelated assays can be misleading.

RT-qPCR measures RNA indirectly via reverse transcription to cDNA. A no-template control asks whether the reaction can yield signal without added target material. A no-RT control instead asks whether a signal in an RNA workflow might arise from DNA rather than RNA-derived cDNA. Neither substitutes for checking the stability of reference targets. A single melting peak can be a useful check in appropriate assays but does not identify the sequence.

Two differences with two separate jobs

With ΔCq = Cq(target) − Cq(reference), the first difference uses an internal reference. ΔΔCq = ΔCq(test) − ΔCq(calibrator) compares the normalised measurements across conditions. Under the ideal assumptions behind the simple comparative model, including near doubling and comparable efficiencies, relative quantity is 2 raised to the power −ΔΔCq. If those assumptions do not hold, the simple result is not a universal correction; the analysis must account for efficiency and be appropriate to the question. MIQE concerns the quality and reporting of qPCR, not a command to use one calculation.

Case 1. The control has ΔCq = 5 and the test has ΔCq = 3. ΔΔCq = −2, corresponding to about four times as much relative target under the simple model. That is a normalised sample-level comparison, not a count of transcripts per cell.

Case 2. The target Cq is unchanged, but the reference Cq drops by one cycle in the test. ΔCq then rises by one. The simple calculation would report a halved target-to-reference ratio even though the target Cq did not move. A changed ratio is real as a description of those inputs; the claim that the target changed requires a defensible reference. Technical wells estimate repeatability, not additional biological sample size.

Panel question and short answer

Question: What does a lower Cq tell you?

Under comparable conditions for the same assay, it is consistent with more starting target. I would also check efficiency, inhibition and the analysis method. A comparison between groups needs an appropriate reference or calibration.

Read further: Bustin et al.: MIQE 2.0 · Livak and Schmittgen: comparative method · Ruijter et al.: baseline and amplification efficiency.

Chapter 05

FISH and spatial evidence

Fluorescence in situ hybridisation uses a labelled probe that recognises a complementary DNA or RNA region in a preserved sample. It retains spatial information: instead of only asking how much signal is associated with a pooled sample, you can ask where detectable target is distributed across cells or compartments. The method measures a signal produced by the probe-target system, not simply a direct count of active cells. Accessibility, background and the imaging conditions can affect signal intensity.

Read absence and overlap cautiously

A signal at the expected location with interpretable controls supports localisation. An apparently negative specimen could also contain inaccessible target, weak signal below detection, or an unsuitable measurement. A positive control supports the ability to detect a target; a background or specificity control addresses a different concern. In multicolour imaging, apparent overlap can reflect limited optical resolution, signal spread, or features at different depths projected onto a single image. Co-localisation can support proximity at the scale of the image. It is not proof of physical binding.

Worked case. Two fictional samples give similar pooled abundance, but in one, signal is distributed across many cells, whereas in the other, a small number of cells account for much of it. Those findings are compatible. Inversely, a change in average FISH intensity need not imply corresponding per-cell changes in target copy number. State which observable was measured in each assay before comparing their results.

Panel question and short answer

Question: Why use FISH if you already have qPCR?

qPCR estimates the abundance of a selected target across a sample. FISH tells me where the detectable target is. Together they can help distinguish a change in abundance from a change in distribution.

Read further: Amann et al.: oligonucleotide probes in intact cells · Fuchs et al.: target accessibility.

Chapter 06

CRISPR-Cas and levels of evidence

Natural system versus research tool

CRISPR-Cas is a family of microbial adaptive immune systems, not a synonym for the Cas9 protein. In the Cas9 example, RNA contributes sequence specificity and a compatible protospacer-adjacent motif (PAM) is part of target recognition. Different Cas systems do not all behave the same way. As a research-tool concept, recruitment of the nuclease, cleavage, stable change at a locus, and the effects of that change are distinct events. Cleavage alone is not an observation of a stable genotype.

What did the measurement actually test?

Detecting Cas9 protein establishes detection of the tool, not a change to the target DNA. A population-average sequence read can miss a mixture or variants outside its coverage. A detected sequence change is not, by itself, proof of loss of function. A knockout is genetic inactivation of a gene; a knockdown reduces expression or gene product without necessarily genetically inactivating the locus. Changes in RNA, protein, activity or phenotype require evidence appropriate to each. If you maintained a pre-existing inducible cell line, that is not the same contribution as constructing it, demonstrating editing, or validating a phenotype.

Panel question and short answer

Question: If you detect Cas9, can you conclude the gene was edited?

No. That shows the tool is present. I would separate evidence that the target sequence changed from evidence that the change altered gene function.

Read further: Jinek et al.: RNA-guided cleavage by Cas9.

Chapter 07

ELISA, cytokines and detection

In a sandwich ELISA, a capture antibody retains the analyte and a second antibody detects a different accessible feature of the captured analyte. The immunoassay generates a signal related to the amount of analyte; the relationship is determined by its calibration curve in the working range, not by an assumption of a linear response at all concentrations. The composition of the sample, interfering substances, background and how well an antibody recognises the intended analyte can affect estimates. A value below the lower quantification limit is not proof of zero; an assay may detect a signal without being able to quantify it reliably.

Concentration in medium versus production by a cell

At one time point, the concentration of detectable cytokine outside the cells reflects multiple processes. These include production, secretion, uptake, loss or breakdown, the number of contributing cells and, where relevant, the medium volume. A measured concentration is not an activity assay. The accessible part of a cytokine measured by the immunoassay need not track biological activity one-to-one.

Worked case. Group A has more detectable cytokine than control at 24 hours, and group B does not have a statistically significant difference from control. Say exactly which comparison supports your claim. Do not infer A differs from B without a direct contrast. Do not infer a higher production rate if you measured the concentration in the medium. An independent readout of cell abundance or an appropriate measure of synthesis or loss could make alternative explanations easier to evaluate.

Panel question and short answer

Question: There is more cytokine in the supernatant. Did the cells produce more?

We found more detectable cytokine in the medium at that time point. Increased production is one possibility, but secretion, loss and the number of contributing cells can also affect that measurement.

Read further: NIH Assay Guidance Manual: immunoassay formats, calibration and range.

Chapter 08

Flow cytometry and Annexin V/PI

What is the denominator of your percentage?

A flow cytometer records light scatter and fluorescence associated with each detected event. An event is not necessarily a single cell: debris and coincident or aggregated cells can enter the stream. A gating hierarchy determines which events are included before a percentage is calculated. Forward and side scatter are useful empirical descriptors, not direct, universal measurements of cell diameter and granularity. The percentage of marker-positive events in a chosen gate does not report the absolute number of such cells unless there is an independent way to estimate counts.

Three controls, three purposes

An unstained sample shows background and autofluorescence. Single-stain controls help estimate spectral spillover for compensation in conventional multicolour cytometry. Fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls contain the rest of the panel but omit the marker of interest, helping set the boundary in the presence of the other markers and their spread. An FMO is not a substitute for a single-stain compensation control. The precise controls you need depend on the measurement and panel.

Annexin V binds exposed phosphatidylserine; PI uptake indicates a compromised membrane under the conditions of this assay. Annexin V-positive/PI-negative events can be consistent with early apoptosis; doubly positive events can fit later apoptosis or other forms of membrane damage. The four quadrants are marker-defined categories, not diagnoses. Additional evidence is needed to distinguish mechanisms. Time-resolved data help describe the sequence of marker changes but do not by themselves establish the mechanism.

Panel question and short answer

Question: Does double positivity prove apoptosis?

No. It shows exposed phosphatidylserine and a compromised membrane in those events. That combination can be consistent with late apoptosis, but I would not assign the mechanism from the two markers alone.

Read further: Koopman et al.: Annexin V readout · Cossarizza et al.: cytometry controls and interpretation.

Chapter 09

Western blot and total protein

From a band to a defensible comparison

In a typical denaturing SDS-PAGE system, migration is mainly associated with molecular size. After separation and transfer, the antibody-based signal indicates recognised material on the membrane. A band near the expected apparent molecular mass is supportive but not proof of identity. Antibody cross-reactivity, alternative protein forms and sample degradation can complicate interpretation. Calling a band stronger is not equivalent to estimating how much more protein is present. Saturation flattens the relationship between amount and signal, and the target and loading reference can saturate at different amounts.

What does Bradford add?

Bradford measures total protein through a dye-binding response, not the abundance of the target protein. Different protein compositions and sample components can affect the relationship between total protein and signal. A measurement before loading does not verify that transfer worked, that detection is linear, or that the loading-control protein is stable. Total protein staining and a stable loading control are possible normalisation references; a denominator has to be justified. If the normalisation reference also changes biologically, the ratio may change even when target abundance does not. An abundance readout is not an activity readout.

Panel question and short answer

Question: What does a darker band tell you?

It can support more detectable target if the band is specific, the signal is in its quantitative range, and the comparison is appropriately normalised. The band alone does not measure activity.

Read further: Towbin et al.: transfer to a membrane · Bradford: total protein measurement · Quantitation and normalisation checks.

Chapter 10

Experimental design and statistical contrasts

Identify the unit, the comparison and the uncertainty

The experimental unit is set by what was independently assigned to a condition or independently sampled for the question of interest. Many events from one cytometry file, wells made from one lysate, or images nested within one donor do not automatically provide that many independent biological observations. A paired design can compare conditions within each donor or sample, preserving the pairing in the analysis. A plot of all measurements may be useful, but treating nested measurements as independent can make uncertainty look unjustifiably small.

A positive control shows whether the study can detect an expected signal under its conditions. A negative control helps evaluate background or specificity. A vehicle control addresses effects of the vehicle. Blocking, randomisation and blinding address different sources of bias or variation. More replicates cannot retrospectively separate two factors that were completely confounded. If every control is in batch 1 and every test in batch 2, the biological comparison and the batch comparison coincide.

Worked case. A differs from control but B does not. Those outcomes result from two different comparisons; the contrast A minus B still needs its own estimate and uncertainty. Similarly, if A goes from 10 to 16 and B from 10 to 14, the observed difference in changes is 2, but the means alone give no measure of its uncertainty. If asking whether groups respond differently over time, make the difference between their changes the target. Report the effect size, uncertainty and design along with a p-value. A non-significant result alone does not establish equivalence.

Panel question and short answer

Question: I tested A against control and B against control. Can I claim A differs from B?

No. I need to estimate A versus B directly, account for the experimental design, and report the size and uncertainty of the difference.

Read further: Lazic et al.: experimental units and pseudoreplication · Gelman and Stern: the difference between significant and not significant.

Chapter 11

Bioinformatics and bulk RNA sequencing

Keep the evidence chain visible

A FASTQ record contains an observed read and base-quality scores. Base-call confidence differs from mapping confidence, which concerns the assignment of a read to a reference location. Alignment or transcript quantification, annotation and aggregation turn reads into a measurement of genes or transcripts. A count matrix without sample metadata does not define a biological comparison. Relevant details include sample provenance, batch, condition and pairing. RNA abundance, variants in DNA and protein activity are different questions even if some file formats or workflows are shared.

Normalisation, PCA and the contrast

Normalisation methods address technical differences such as library scale or composition under stated assumptions. They do not make all biological or technical variation disappear. PCA on suitable transformed values summarises major sources of sample variation. If samples separate by condition, check whether batch, donor or cell composition matches that split. If condition and batch are completely confounded, the data alone cannot identify their separate effects. Transformed expression for visualisation is not automatically valid input for a count-based model.

Worked case. A gene has an estimated log₂ fold change of +1 for test versus control. The estimated ratio is two to one in that direction; it is not a statement about measurement precision or the probability of being a genuine discovery. An FDR threshold concerns the expected proportion of false discoveries among discoveries under the procedure's assumptions, not the probability that a particular gene is a false hit. Bulk RNA-seq averages across a mixture: an apparent upregulation could reflect a changed cell mixture rather than more RNA of that gene within each cell type.

Make your contribution concrete

Explain what data came in, what decision you made about their quality or comparison, and what output someone else could reproduce. Keep a link between groups in the sample table, clarity on which direction a comparison is written, and a record of relevant reference, annotation and analysis choices. Be clear whether an example came from work, a course, or a project you are proposing to do.

Panel question and short answer

Question: The PCA separates the two conditions. What would you check?

I’d check whether condition overlaps with batch or sample composition and examine the sample-level metadata. PCA describes variation; it does not tell me what caused it.

Read further: Bioconductor: RNA-seq exploratory and differential analysis workflow · DESeq2 documentation: model design, counts and confounding.

Chapter 12

Microbiome data, bacteriophages and AMR

Microbiome: know the denominator

A relative abundance is a share of a total. A taxon can lose share without losing absolute abundance if the total increases. A library's read depth does not independently establish microbial load in the original sample. Extraction, library construction, classification and reference representation can also affect how taxa are represented. Amplicon and shotgun surveys give different kinds of evidence; taxonomic membership is not itself proof of which biological function is active or of a causal host-microbe relationship.

Phages: separate states and methods of inference

Lytic infection produces progeny and ultimately leads to lysis. Temperate phages can also establish a state where their genome persists in a bacterium as a prophage, often integrated but sometimes maintained outside the chromosome. A prophage-like sequence by itself does not establish that the element is complete or producing infectious progeny. Computational host prediction proposes an association at a given taxonomic resolution; a prediction at genus level does not demonstrate productive infection of a specified strain. Absence from a catalogue or database can mean incomplete sampling. Look for a held-out evaluation and ask how the task defined a correct prediction.

Resistance is not the only reason cells survive

Resistance is associated with growth at higher antibiotic concentrations, reflected in a shift in MIC under comparable conditions. Clinical resistance categories, where applicable, are a separate interpretation using appropriate criteria. Tolerance concerns slower killing without a corresponding increase in MIC. Persistence describes heterogeneous tolerance in which a subpopulation survives bactericidal exposure longer without increased resistance. The mere observation of a surviving subpopulation does not distinguish persistence from heteroresistance, which involves a subpopulation with higher resistance. State whether the underlying readout measured growth inhibition or time-dependent survival.

Panel question and short answer

Question: A bacterium was predicted to be a host. What does that mean?

The prediction supports a possible association at the reported taxonomic level. It does not demonstrate that a particular strain supports productive infection.

Read further: Vandeputte et al.: microbiome profiling and microbial load · Roux et al.: evaluating phage-host predictions · Balaban et al.: definitions of persistence and related terms.

Chapter 13

Presenting your own research

Keep the short answer short

Start with the biological question, not a list of techniques. Define the experimental model in terms the panel can follow. Say what you personally did, report one result with its comparator, then identify one thing the result cannot establish. If the work is unfinished, describe a decision you can defend and the evidence behind it; do not imply that a hoped-for endpoint was achieved. If asked what to do differently, name a weakness that changed the inference and connect the improvement to that weakness.

Train three versions of the same example

In 20 seconds, name the question, your part and the result. In about one minute, add the key control and competing interpretation. When challenged, answer the exact question first: for example, specify the denominator of a percentage or give the actual statistical comparison. Then explain which observation would make you revise your interpretation. Pick one figure you can describe aloud, including axes, units, independent observations and uncertainty.

A good reason to join a PhD programme is specific to the work and training you want. Say what kind of analysis you would like to own and what kind of support would help you do it independently. If a topic is new to you, state your transferable experience and the question you want to learn to address rather than implying hands-on experience you do not have. Follow the timing and format in your invitation rather than assuming every interview round is identical.

Panel question and short answer

Question: Tell us about a result you can defend.

We asked whether… My part was… Compared with…, I observed… That supports…, but this measurement alone does not distinguish…
